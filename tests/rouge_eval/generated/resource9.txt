This resource provides guidance on using inclusive language when communicating with or about disabled people, aimed at writers, communicators, and anyone engaging with disability topics. It emphasizes respectful terminology, advising the use of "disabled people" rather than collective labels like "the disabled," and encourages avoiding medical or negative terms that reinforce stereotypes. The guidance highlights the importance of positive language, such as referring to "wheelchair users" instead of "confined to a wheelchair," and cautions against everyday phrases that may unintentionally carry negative connotations. Practical behavioral tips include speaking directly to disabled individuals without patronizing or speaking for them, and maintaining a normal tone of voice. Overall, the resource promotes language that respects disabled people as active individuals with control over their lives.
